## The situation

Atom teaches people how to form habits and uses guided meditation as its first application. The case-study brief was to design a habit tracker that could broaden the product’s value and make the paid version more compelling.

The work took place during an intensive three-month Product Owner programme and covered the full product cycle rather than a single design deliverable.

## The product question

A tracker can create more work than motivation. The central question was not “How do we log habits?” but “How can tracking reinforce a user’s intention without disrupting the calm, guided experience that made Atom useful?”

## How I approached it

- Framed the opportunity through product strategy and target behaviours.
- Connected the feature to a sustainable business model and paid-product value.
- Mapped journeys and moments where reflection or reinforcement would be useful.
- Explored flows in low fidelity before producing a clickable high-fidelity prototype.
- Broke the concept into a simulated backlog and prioritised an initial release.
- Defined leading and lagging indicators for adoption, retention, and habit completion.
- Used feedback from product experts and practitioners to challenge the concept.

## The proposed experience

The feature allows users to set an intention, record a habit, and see fulfilment over time. Rather than relying on streak anxiety, the concept uses small moments of reflection and progress to support the behaviour Atom is teaching.

## What I learned

The project reinforced that feature adoption and product value are not the same thing. A successful tracker would need to improve users’ ability to form habits—not merely increase the number of taps inside the app.

## Tools

Figma, Miro, product strategy frameworks, backlog refinement, and KPI definition.

## What I would add now

The concept should be tested with real Atom users. The next evidence should cover comprehension, emotional response to missed habits, repeat usage, and willingness to pay.
